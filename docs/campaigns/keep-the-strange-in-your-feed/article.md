# Keep the strange in your feed.

By Cody Haring / Ultramonkeydog Studios

A creature-design exercise should not disappear because you missed a post. Neither should a strange little studio require a daily attendance ritual. If something here gets under your skin, there should be a way to return without hunting through an algorithm’s leftovers.

There is now a direct address for the studio journal: https://ultramonkeydog-studios.vercel.app/feed.xml. Add it to an RSS reader. New public dispatches appear there with a short summary and a link to the whole piece. No studio account. No email signup. You choose when to open one.

## Follow the work, not a performance of being busy

The journal is where I can slow down enough to explain a choice. Why a monster’s shadow should tell you something before its texture does. Why an evolution should change a decision, not only a damage number. Why a system that produces a result should leave enough evidence to argue with it.

Those are different questions. They deserve more room than a feature list, and they do not need to become a constant stream of announcements. A finished design exercise can be useful even while the game that prompted the question is still being made.

Start with the four creature-design tests in Silhouette Before Surface. Take one body through shadow, action, consequence and surface. Keep the detail that carries its identity. Reject the polish that hides the thing a player needs to read. That is a concrete way into the studio’s taste—not a demand to understand every project at once.

## A doorway, not a release promise

The feed follows published writing. It does not distribute game builds, open a beta or turn an in-development project into a released product. Each article keeps its own boundaries. A design sketch is still a design sketch; a runtime capture is still evidence of the thing it actually shows.

I’m Cody Haring, the self-taught creator directing Ultramonkeydog Studios. AI helps me cross production disciplines. It does not decide which creature belongs, which idea deserves another pass or what earns publication. This address follows the public work, not the private machinery behind it.

## Set it once. Leave when you want.

Copy the feed address into your reader’s Add feed or Subscribe field. The reader checks for updates; you do not need to send me anything. If opening the address shows XML, that is the subscription file doing its job. The same dispatches remain readable in the web archive.

This is not a private channel or a promise of anonymous browsing. Your reader and the site’s hosting provider may process requests under their own policies. The studio asks for no subscription email here, and following does not enroll you in a mailing list. Remove the feed from your reader whenever it stops being useful.

Keep the work that interests you. Let the rest pass. There is room for strange things without asking them to shout every day.
